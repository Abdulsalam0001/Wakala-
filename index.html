The problem

Cross-border payments in Africa still route through someone else's currency.

Every transfer between neighbouring markets is typically priced, held and cleared in US dollars first — adding fees, delay and a rate nobody controls, even when both sides just want their own money to move.

How Wakala works

One account. Your own currency. Every step in between removed.

01 — Fund

Load your wallet in local currency

Top up in shillings, francs or naira through mobile money, bank transfer or a verified Wakala agent near you — the same way you already pay for everything else.

02 — Convert

Cross the border on one direct rail

Wakala prices your transfer straight between the two local currencies, at a rate you see before you confirm — never routed through the dollar.

03 — Spend or receive

Money lands ready to use

The recipient gets local currency in their own Wakala wallet, ready to spend, withdraw through an agent, or send onward — usually in minutes.

For investors

Building the local-currency rail underneath Africa's fastest-growing corridors.

  • 01

    Mobile money is the default, not the alternative

    Across our five launch markets, mobile money is already the primary way most people move day-to-day money — Wakala meets people on rails they already trust.

  • 02

    Intra-African trade and travel are accelerating

    The African Continental Free Trade Area is lowering barriers to trade between exactly the markets Wakala connects first — demand for direct local-currency settlement is growing ahead of the infrastructure to support it.

  • 03

    Distribution through people, not just an app

    Our agent network — the "wakala" the company is named for — gives us cash-in, cash-out and trust in communities a purely digital app can't reach alone.

  • 04

    The app and eSIM extend the same wallet

    Connectivity is bundled into the same product, deepening daily usage and giving Wakala a second, defensible revenue line beyond FX.
